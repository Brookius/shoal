Dark mode, app-wide — token set & decisions

Harbour Night · .theme-harbour moved from #footage-modal to <body>

Option A was already locked; this pass asks what breaks when it stops being modal-scoped. Six things did. Every ratio below is computed against the real tokens (WCAG 2.x relative luminance), and every perceptual distance is ΔEok in OKLab — used only for separation questions, never as a stand-in for contrast. The rule I held throughout: hue identity is fixed, lightness and saturation may move. Sibling files render these tokens on the real screens.

Decision 1 — --accent at app scale: the value holds

The modal only ever put --accent on two rungs. At app scale it has to clear all five. It does, with room:

Rung-lowest-low-base-high-highestverdict
#74B3D8 as text8.406.697.485.964.91min 4.91 — AA on every rung

So the answer to "is #74B3D8 a genuine choice or a modal patch" is: a genuine choice. It needs no change. Worth noting why it survives where the light token doesn't — light --accent #4A90B8 is 3.27:1 as text and is still a tracked open item; the dark theme has already solved for itself the exact problem light mode hasn't.

But it exposed one real bug, which app scale is what surfaces. --accent is documented as interactive ink, and is nearly always used that way — yet it also fills five things. Four are bare bars, dots and underlines with nothing on them (.tc-fill, #lf-ble-progress-fill, the track's stop dot, .df-seg-btn.active::after) and are unaffected. The fifth, .dD-select-box.checked, fills with var(--accent) and strokes a white tick across it.

On light that's 3.52:1 — thin but a graphical object, so it passes 1.4.11. On dark the lift makes it 2.29:1 and it fails. The fix is a one-line component change, not a token change: that checkbox should fill with --accent-fill (the pair that owns white ink) rather than --accent. Every other --accent fill stays as-is.

Decision 1c — --accent-text: the split collapses on dark

Light mode split the accent in two on 2026-08-04, mid-way through this pass: --accent keeps strokes, tints and non-text fills, and a darkened --accent-text #397191 takes text, which is how the long-tracked 3.27:1 cerulean-as-text problem was finally closed. It applies to 65 declarations.

A theme that doesn't redeclare it inherits that light value, and it fails on every dark rung:

Value-lowest-low-base-high-highest
#397191 (light, unthemed)3.612.883.212.562.11
#74B3D8 (dark)8.406.697.485.964.91

The dark value is simply --accent itself: the two tokens are identical on dark, and deliberately so. The split exists because on a light page the value that reads well as a stroke is too light to read as text, so text has to be darkened away from it. On a dark page the lift already carried --accent past the text floor in the same direction — there is no second value left to derive. Declaring --accent-text anyway, rather than leaving it to inherit, is the whole point: the token has to be named in the theme block or it silently keeps the light hex. This is the same failure mode as the 40 hardcoded light hexes in decision 6, arriving through a token instead of a literal, and it is worth watching for on every future token added to :root — a theme block is not automatically kept in step with the palette it overrides.

Decision 1b — --accent-fill stays constant, and that survives dark. Two other fills don't.

"Primary actions look identical in every theme" is a stated rule, so --accent-fill #407DA0 was checked rather than assumed. It holds: 3.40:1 against the dark card (clears 1.4.11's 3:1 for a control) and white ink on it is still 4.51:1. Unchanged.

The same test fails for the other two semantic fills, which is what forces decision 2b below:

FillLight hexvs dark cardResult
--accent-fill#407DA03.40survives — stays constant
--danger (light)#B0492E2.80a dark rust button on a dark canvas
--success (light)#3B76502.84same

The difference is simply that --accent-fill is a mid-tone and the other two are dark-tones. That's why the constancy rule can hold for one and not the others — worth recording, because "fill pairs are constant across themes" reads like it should apply to all three.

Decision 2 — --danger: adjusted, #D26A4D → #E96E4C

The shipped dark --danger was verified against the modal's rungs only. Across the full ladder it under-runs:

Value-lowest-low (cards)-base (page)-high
#D26A4D shipped5.414.314.813.83
#E96E4C locked6.394.965.694.41

Sized against --surface-low, not the page — inverting light mode's choice, and deliberately. Delete controls and SAC-high figures sit on cards, and on dark the card rung is lighter than the page (#182827 vs #111E1D), so the card is the worst realistic case here where the page was the worst case in light. The -high residual at 4.41 is the same class of accepted shortfall light mode records at 4.30–4.32, for the same reason: that rung carries troughs and tracks, not status text. -highest is excluded by the existing "full ink only" rule.

The interesting part isn't the lift, it's what the lift collided with. Holding hue and chroma and just raising lightness lands on #D66E51 — which clears the floor but sits ΔEok 0.038 from --type-Reef. That is closer than any two colours this palette deliberately ships: light mode's own --warn/--danger pair, the tightest intentional gap in the system, is 0.050 after a dedicated fix. A reserved semantic landing inside a categorical ramp member is exactly the class-2/class-3 blur the three-class model exists to prevent.

Why it happens: in light mode Reef and danger are separated almost entirely by lightness (ΔEok 0.125; Reef sits at L 0.655, danger at L 0.537) — they were never far apart in hue, 15°. Dark mode's contrast floor forces danger up into the readable band, which is precisely where Reef already lives. The orange band gets compressed and three tokens want it: Reef, --danger, --warn.

Resolution: spend the remaining budget on chroma rather than more lightness. #E96E4C holds hue at 36.5° and raises chroma to 0.160, which restores ΔEok 0.060 to Reef and 0.061 to --warn — both at or above the system's own demonstrated tolerance. Pushing lightness further instead was tried and rejected: it buys Reef separation only by walking into --warn (at L 0.72 the two are ΔEok 0.016 apart, i.e. the same colour).

Decision 2a — --warn, --success, the violets and --gold: no change needed

All were re-measured across the full ladder rather than assumed. Against --surface-high, the worst rung that carries status text: --warn 5.39, --success 5.25, --violet-deep 4.77, --violet-soft 6.40, --gold 6.90. All pass. --gold is untouched per brief.

One thing I expected to have to fix and didn't — worth recording because the reasoning inverts light mode's. Dark --warn #E98A6A and --danger sit only 2.8° of hue apart with warn the less saturated of the two: geometrically the identical shape as the pre-2026-07-30 light pair that a whole correction pass was spent unpicking. But measured, they're ΔEok 0.088 apart — comfortably better than the 0.050 light ships. The separation is doing its work through lightness (0.727 vs 0.641), the axis light mode couldn't use: on a light page both had to be dark enough to clear 4.5:1, which pinned them to the same lightness and left hue as the only lever. A dark canvas hands that lever back. So dark mode gets to solve this the way light mode wanted to and couldn't, and --warn needs no hue rotation here.

Decision 3 — the dive-type ramp: 7 lifted, 3 left alone

Method matters more than the values here, because three different methods give three different ramps.

MethodWhat it doesTightest pair (ΔEok)Verdict
Light ramp (baseline)— what ships today —0.053 Shore/Reefthe bar to match
A · _hexLerp toward whitethe house method, used for the surface rungs and --profile-*0.033 Night/Caverejected
B · uniform lightnessflatten all ten to one L so hue alone discriminates0.037 Shore/Wreckrejected
C · minimum lift + marginraise each member only as far as the floors need0.053 Shore/Reeflocked

A was rejected because lerping toward white desaturates, and it desaturates hardest exactly the members that need the most lift — Night lost 33% of its chroma, Wreck 28%, Wall 27% — collapsing Night/Cave to 0.033, tighter than any pair the light ramp ships. This is the one place the project's own house derivation is the wrong tool: it was chosen for neutrals, where losing chroma costs nothing, and a categorical ramp is the opposite case.

B was rejected more narrowly. Flattening to one lightness sounds right for a categorical scale — let hue do all the work — but it discards lightness relationships that are currently carrying distinctions. Shore and Wreck are 22° apart in hue and told apart in light almost entirely by Wreck being much darker; at a common lightness they converge to 0.037.

C keeps relative lightness ordering intact by lifting each member only to the floor plus a margin, so Wreck stays darker than Shore, Night stays darker than Cave, and the ramp's internal structure is preserved. It lands at exactly the light ramp's own tightest pair — 0.053, same pair — meaning the dark ramp is as distinct as the one already shipping, not merely adequate.

Three members needed nothing at all: Shore, Drift and Reef already clear both dark floors at their :root values. They're deliberately omitted from the theme block rather than restated — a restated-but-identical value is the first step toward the four-contradictory-copies history this ramp already has.

Decision 3b — --on-type, a new pair the ramp always needed

Lifting the ramp breaks white ink on it. Two components draw text directly on a type colour — .dD-spine span (the timeline's vertical caps) and .lf-type-chip.sel (the log form's selected dive-type chip) — and both hardcode #fff. On the lifted ramp that runs 1.6–2.4:1.

So the ramp gets a named foreground partner like every other container colour: --on-type — #FFFFFF in light, #091010 in dark, 4.80:1 minimum across all ten. Conceptually this is the ink↔paper swap reaching the last part of the palette it hadn't: on dark the type chip becomes a bright block with dark type on it.

Measuring this turned up a pre-existing light-mode failure, unrelated to dark mode. White on the light ramp already fails for three members: Shore 3.21, Drift 2.84, Reef 3.30 — the spine's own type word, which the history design brief calls "the legend… no separate key needed", is under AA on those three today. It's the same three that fail the stats bar, and for the same reason: they're the lightest members. Recorded, not fixed here — it's a light-mode bug and this pass is scoped to dark. But --on-type is the seam it would be fixed through.

Decision 4b — colourblind distinctness, previously unverified

Flagged as an open question and never measured, so it was measured. Under simulated deuteranopia the dark ramp's tightest pair is Wreck/Muck at 0.014; the light ramp's is the same pair at 0.011. Protanopia: 0.015 dark. Dark is marginally better than light, and neither is good — Wreck/Muck and Shore/Reef are close to indistinguishable without colour vision in both themes. That's a pre-existing property of the ramp, not something dark mode introduces, and it is not solvable by lightness/saturation moves alone. Recorded here so the next person doesn't have to re-derive it; the real fix is non-colour redundancy, which the spine already has (the type word is written on it).

Decision 5 — IUCN chips: all eight ranks, and two were broken

The shipped .theme-harbour block covers CR, EN, VU and EW — the four the footage modal happens to render. At app scope the species album and log form show all eight. Checking them found two real problems, not just four missing declarations.

RankDark treatmentratioStatus

CR failed AA at the value that shipped. "CR holds rank through weight + stronger fill on dark, not through darkness" is the right instinct, but the specific implementation of "stronger fill" — a brighter violet tint at α0.22 — lifts the chip's background toward its own text and lands CR at 3.74:1. Dropping the alpha fixes contrast (α0.10 → 4.59) but then CR is less filled than EN and the rank signal inverts.

Resolution: on dark, "stronger fill" means a deeper fill, not a brighter one — rgba(9,16,16,0.55), the theme's own deepest tone, which pushes the chip away from its violet ink instead of toward it. CR reads 6.16:1, still visibly denser and heavier than EN's light tint, with the rank order intact. On dark, emphasis inverts direction: adding weight to something means taking light away from it, not adding more.

Decision 5b — VU comes off gold, and EW/EX pay for it

Shoal logs sightings, so EW (extinct in the wild) and EX (extinct) can never legitimately be recorded. Checked rather than assumed: of the 1,275 rows in data/species-db.js, zero carry either status (LC 709, blank 376, VU 60, EN 39, NT 34, DD 33, CR 22). Two dead chip treatments, and the tracked "gold leaks into VU" item wanting a home.

VU takes the treatment EW was holding — full neutral ink on a stronger tint and border — not EX's solid pale fill, which reads far louder than CR or EN and would invert the rank order visually. And measuring it turned up something the gold-leak item never recorded:

VULightratioDarkratio
today (gold)#a07a10 on rgba(212,168,67,.15)3.33#D9B45C on rgba(217,180,92,.14)5.82
proposedvar(--text) on rgba(28,48,48,.12)10.30var(--text) on rgba(237,230,218,.12)8.86

Light-mode VU is 3.33:1 today — it fails AA outright. The open item recorded this as a token-hygiene problem ("reads as compliant to a var() grep, isn't"); it is also a live contrast failure that nobody had put a number on. Fixing the leak fixes the contrast as a side effect.

It also settles a place where the doc and the code have quietly disagreed. The strict rule says "VU/NT/LC IUCN tiers stay plain Deep Water text (only CR/EN get violet) — too many dived species are VU; colouring them dilutes the signal." The code gives VU gold and NT/LC --text-muted: neither matches. The proposal puts VU on plain --text exactly as written, and leaves NT/LC/DD muted — which draws the hierarchy along the line IUCN itself draws, since CR/EN/VU are the three "threatened" categories and NT/LC/DD are not:

  • CR — deep violet, deep fill, weight 800
  • EN — soft violet, light tint
  • VU — no hue, but full ink and a stronger border: still marked, no longer competing
  • NT / LC / DD — muted ink, faint tint: recede

VU is comfortably distinct from the tier below it (ΔEok 0.228 light / 0.155 dark against --text-muted) and from the violets above (0.183 vs EN, 0.266 vs CR). It measures higher contrast than CR — 8.86 vs 6.16 — but contrast isn't salience: a neutral chip among violet ones reads as unmarked, not emphasised. Worth watching on a real screen, and the fill alpha is the dial if it needs calming.

Decision 5c — the deleted rules become a fallback, because two statuses have none

Found while confirming EW/EX were unused: two species render with no chip styling at all. The DB contains one LR/nt and one NA — legacy IUCN codes that survived the AphiaID audit. album.js:269 builds the class as 'iucn-' + iucn, so those become .iucn-LR/nt (not even a valid selector — the /) and .iucn-NA (simply undefined). Both fall through to the base .iucn-badge, which sets no background, no border and no colour — bare text where every neighbour is a chip.

So rather than delete EW/EX outright, their slot becomes a default: the neutral treatment moves onto the base .iucn/.iucn-badge classes, and the six real ranks override it. Every unrecognised status — LR/nt, NA, and a future EW/EX if a species batch ever introduces one — lands on the neutral chip instead of nothing. This is strictly better than either keeping or deleting the dead rules: it fixes a live bug, removes two dead ones, and closes the silent-fallthrough class of failure rather than one instance of it.

--gold itself is untouched and now has no call site outside its charter — the achievement badge it was always reserved for. That closes the open item rather than inheriting it.

Decision 6 — color: var(--text) on the theme class

Present, first line, and the reason is worth keeping next to it rather than in a changelog: a descendant with no explicit color inherits body's computed value, which is the light ink resolved before the theme class ever applied. Token overrides don't retroactively re-resolve it. At modal scope this cost one unreadable title; at <body> scope it would be most of the app.

App-wide scope adds a second version of the same trap that the modal never hit: 40 declarations in css/styles.css hardcode a light-mode hex (#1C3030, #FAF6F1, #F5EBD8, rgba(28,48,48,…)) instead of a token. Every one is invisible to a theme class for exactly the same reason. Two are already known and patched in the shipped block (the coral hover literals); .iucn-EX's #1C3030 fill was a third, now deleted outright by decision 5b rather than themed. The rest are an implementation-time sweep, not a colour decision — but they are the thing most likely to make an otherwise-correct theme look broken. --accent-text (decision 1c) is the same failure arriving through a token instead of a literal, which is the more dangerous version: a literal at least looks wrong when you read it.

Decision 7 — dive-profile chart: two derivations invert

These tokens are read by renderProfileChart through getComputedStyle, so they re-theme automatically — which means they re-theme automatically into nonsense unless they're re-derived. Both of the ones defined as "lerp toward black/white" break, and they break in opposite directions.

TokenLightDarkWhy
--profile-calm#89B7D1#89B7D1 kept6.15 / 5.77 over the dark fills — already right
--profile-warn#E0734F#E0734F kept4.24 / 3.98 — already right
--profile-deco#6A2C1C#E14752"danger darkened 40%" = 1.45:1, invisible
--profile-fill-top#C0D8E6#1F3337"accent + 65% white" = a glowing white block
--profile-fill-bottom#65A1C3#233740same

The water column is a lerp toward white, so on dark it has to become a lerp toward the canvas instead — --accent 86% / 76% toward --surface-base / --surface-lowest. The gradient's meaning is unchanged (lighter at the surface, deepening with depth); only its anchor moves from white to the dark canvas. Each of the three curve colours clears 3:1 against both ends.

Locked-deco is the interesting one. It's defined as --danger darkened 40% toward black, encoding "worse than warn" as darker — which works on a light page and is exactly backwards on a dark one, where darker means less visible. Every darkened candidate failed: 2.04–2.69:1 against the water column, i.e. a state the chart exists to make unmissable, rendered nearly invisible. On dark, "deeper" has to mean more saturated and pushed redder, not darker: #E14752 sits 18° red of --profile-warn at chroma 0.19, clears 3.29 / 3.09 against the fills, and holds ΔEok 0.088 from warn — a wider gap than the two have in light mode. Same principle as CR and EX above, a third time: on dark, intensity is the axis that darkness was in light.

What needed no change at all

  • The surface ladder. All five rungs re-measured at app scope; --text runs 9.06–15.49 across them, matching the shipped figures exactly.
  • The mobile bottom nav and desktop sidebar. Both are already permanently dark and already run on --accent-on-dark #97C6E2, a constant derived from dark-mode --accent in the first place. An app-wide dark theme is the case they were accidentally built for; they need nothing.
  • --gold, and every ramp hue's identity. Out of scope per brief and untouched — maximum hue drift across the ten types is 0.6°.
  • --text-dim at 2.76:1 on --surface-highest. Below AA, and correct — the pair is documented as decorative-only and cannot pass by design.

Residuals, disclosed

  • --danger is 4.41:1 on --surface-high, under 4.5. Same class of accepted shortfall as light mode's 4.30–4.32 on the same rung, and for the same reason: that rung carries troughs and tracks, not status text.
  • --danger to --type-Reef is ΔEok 0.060 against light's 0.125. Improved from 0.038 but still tighter than light, because dark mode's contrast floor compresses the orange band. Above the system's own demonstrated tolerance (0.050) but worth knowing.
  • Light-mode white-on-spine fails for Shore (3.21), Drift (2.84) and Reef (3.30), and the light stats bars still fail for the same three. Found by this pass, out of its scope.
  • The 40 hardcoded light hexes in css/styles.css are an implementation-time sweep this mockup can't stand in for.
