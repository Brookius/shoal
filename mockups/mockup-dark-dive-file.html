Hooked in on the ridge

Ripping current on the north side — we dropped early, swam hard for the ridge and hooked in behind the rocks before it could push us off. Worth it. The trevally were working a bait ball right on the lip and the whole thing just hung there for twenty minutes.

A hawksbill came through low over the coral about halfway, completely unbothered, and went straight past Yasmine at arm's length. Unhooked at 80 bar and let it carry us round the corner into the sand.

The profile chart is the only place a token's own derivation went wrong rather than just its value. --profile-fill-top/-bottom are defined as --accent lerped toward white, and --profile-deco as --danger darkened toward black. Carried onto a dark canvas unchanged, the water column becomes a glowing white block (10.36:1 against the card) and locked-deco becomes invisible (1.45:1). Both are re-derived above against the theme's own anchors instead of white/black. The chart reads these through getComputedStyle, so it re-themes on its own — which is exactly why a wrong value here would ship silently.

Locked-deco is worth its own line. "Darker = worse" is a light-mode idea; on a dark canvas darker means less visible, so every darkened candidate landed at 2.04–2.69:1 over the water column — a state whose entire job is to be unmissable, rendered nearly invisible. On dark, "deeper" has to mean more saturated and pushed redder: #E14752 sits 18° red of --profile-warn at chroma 0.19, clears 3.29 / 3.09 against the two fill stops, and holds ΔEok 0.088 from warn — a wider gap than the two have in light mode.

The tank gauge has a logic bug on dark, not a colour one. The start number is coloured --text unconditionally, and the code says why: dark ink reads on both the empty body (11.09:1) and the blue fill (3.94:1), so it's safe wherever the fill edge lands. That premise inverts here — --text is now the light ink, giving 10.98:1 on the empty body but 1.84:1 on the fill. A nearly-full tank (220→190, described in that same comment as the common case) would swallow its own start number. The end number is fine, because its existing "is it on the fill" guard happens to still ask the right question.

The fix is to apply that same predicate to both numbers rather than to one — the ink for each depends only on whether it sits on fill or body, and light mode merely got away with skipping the test for one of them. Switching the fill to --accent-fill instead would let both inks sit on one mid-tone (3.63 / 3.40), but that is thinner than the per-position answer and changes how the gauge looks in both themes.

Unchanged and correct: the vis and temp gradient bars keep their baked literals — they encode water clarity and temperature, not UI state — and gain only a box-shadow ring so their dark ends don't merge with the canvas. The hero's own gradient scrim already anchors on near-black and needed nothing.
