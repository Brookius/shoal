# v2.72 Brief — Notes as a journal: capture, headline, and read-back

> **Status: ready to build.** Persona-driven, derived from the June 2026
> target-user research (`research/target-user-research.md`) and the chat arc
> that followed. Two commits: phase `2.72` (capture side) and phase `2.73`
> (read-back side), closing stamp `2.74`. Ships independently of any other
> work; touches no sync/backend logic. The web build stays the source of
> truth — every change is wrapper-agnostic (`isShell()` not consulted).

---

## 1. The job to be done

The research is emphatic and multi-sourced (Candice Landau built a whole paper
product around it; the Girls that Scuba reviewer fills logbook margins) that for
the target persona — the marine-life-curious diver who journals — **the notes
field is the most important field in the log**, more important than SAC or even
depth. The current app contradicts that on both sides of the loop:

1. **Capture.** "Dive notes" is buried as one of five fields in the last
   accordion section, `cs-notes` ("Notes & sign-off"), sandwiched between
   logistics (liveaboard, buddy) and BSAC paperwork (instructor, cert number).
   Housing the journal under the same roof as a cert number frames writing about
   a dive as administrative box-ticking. Placeholder text — *"Conditions,
   highlights, skills practiced, any incidents…"* — is an incident report, not
   an invitation to write.

2. **Read-back.** What she writes is nearly invisible afterwards. In the dive
   file it's a third tab she must choose; in the history timeline it doesn't
   appear at all (depth/time/vis + a species thumb). Flicking a paper logbook,
   you scan the notes to remember what a dive *was* — the timeline gives her no
   equivalent.

This brief fixes both: it makes notes a first-class capture surface with an
optional headline, and it surfaces that writing back in the timeline and dive
file so the app rewards re-opening.

**Out of persona-scope but worth stating:** none of this taxes a non-journaling
user (e.g. Luke). Title and notes stay optional; every addition has a graceful
empty state and falls back to today's behaviour when blank.

## 2. Hard scope boundaries (do not exceed)

- **No quick-log / minimal-entry mode.** Deliberately deferred pending real user
  feedback — every field is already optional and you can save partial and return
  later, which already avoids the "forced through sections" anti-pattern. Do not
  add a stripped-back log path in this brief.
- **One new data field only: `title`** (optional string). No other data-model
  change. No multi-cylinder, no per-sighting photo, nothing else from the
  research backlog.
- **No dive-file redesign.** The marine-first hero, stat band, two-column desktop
  layout, and mobile segmented tabs all stay. Notes is *repositioned and
  restyled*, not restructured.
- **No build tools / no ES modules** — classic ordered `<script src>`, functions
  stay global, inline handlers unchanged (carry-forward).
- **`esc()` everywhere.** Title and notes are user free-text → every new
  `innerHTML` interpolation goes through `esc()` (app.js). Title also gets
  shape-coerced in `frontmatterToDive`.
- **No autocomplete on `title`.** It's unique per dive; it must NOT be added to
  the AC cache keys or bootstrap.

## 3. Phase 2.72 — capture side

### 3a. Reorder the form: split the grab-bag

Today's log-form accordion (`index.html`) is six sections:

```
A cs-dive        (open by default)
B cs-conditions
C cs-profile
D cs-equipment
E cs-marine      (open by default)
F cs-notes       "Notes & sign-off"  ← liveaboard, buddy, dive notes, instructor, cert
```

Split F into the **narrative** and the **leftovers**, and pair the narrative with
Marine life so the two "story" sections are adjacent:

```
A cs-dive        (open by default)
B cs-conditions
C cs-profile
D cs-equipment
E cs-marine      (open by default)
F cs-notes       "Notes"             ← NEW: title field + dive notes (narrative only)
G cs-signoff     "Buddy & sign-off"  ← liveaboard, buddy, instructor, cert
```

- Marine life (what I saw) → Notes (what it was like) now sit back to back: the
  emotional cluster, separated from the technical block above it.
- Section title "Notes" (not "Journal") to stay grounded; the journal *feel*
  comes from the title field, the prompt, and the serif read-back, not a rename.
  (`Journal` is an acceptable alt if it tests better later — not this pass.)
- `cs-signoff` keeps liveaboard/buddy/instructor/cert. Renaming it "Buddy &
  sign-off" is honest about its contents. *Deferred, not in scope:* buddy and
  liveaboard arguably belong in the Dive section — leave them in G for now to
  keep the diff small.
- Accordion stays **exclusive** (one open at a time); seven sections is fine.
- Each section keeps its 3-state summary chip. New `cs-notes` chip logic:
  - empty (no title, no notes) → `Expand`
  - title set → show the title, truncated ~24 chars
  - notes set, no title → `✓ Notes`
- **Edit modal parity:** the edit modal mirrors these sections (built in
  `openEdit`, app.js). Apply the identical split + title field. It continues to
  open with Marine life expanded.

### 3b. The Notes section contents

```
[ Title        ]  optional, single line, maxlength ~50, placeholder e.g.
                  "Name this dive (optional)"
[ Dive notes   ]  textarea — bigger canvas (taller default, comfortable
                  line-height), placeholder reframed to a narrative invitation,
                  e.g. "What do you want to remember about this dive?"
```

- Bigger canvas: raise the textarea default height and `line-height`; optionally
  auto-grow. The point is it must not read as an afterthought box.
- Wire both `title` and `notes` into `saveDive` / edit-save.

### 3c. Data model: the `title` field

- **Dive object:** add `title: ''`.
- **`generateFrontmatter` (markdown.js):** emit `title:` into the YAML block
  (quoted/escaped, same treatment as other free-text). When present, also render
  it as a top-level heading in the human-readable body (e.g. `# {title}`) so it
  reads well in Obsidian.
- **`frontmatterToDive` (markdown.js):** read `title`, coerce to string, default
  `''` (join with the existing shape-coercion pass).
- **Footage sidecar / uid / trip grouping:** untouched. `title` is dive-local.

### 3d. Beautify the notes block (rendered by `renderDiveDetail`, history.js)

Build a small helper `_notesBlockHtml(d)` returning the journal-styled block,
used by both the desktop and mobile placements (see 4c):

- **With a title:** serif **upright** heading (the title) → `site · date` meta
  line (mono, dim) → hairline rule → serif **upright** body (the notes).
- **Without a title:** a quiet `NOTES` kicker (feather glyph + mono, accent) →
  serif upright body.
- All interpolation through `esc()`.

## 4. Phase 2.73 — read-back side

### 4a. The glimpse helper (the fallback chain)

Add `_diveGlimpse(d)` → `{ kind, text }`:

1. `d.title` (trimmed) → `{ kind:'title', text }`
2. else `d.notes` (trimmed) → first sentence (cut at `. ! ?`); if that exceeds
   ~70 chars or has no terminator, cut at the last word boundary before ~70 and
   append `…`. Strip leading markdown (`#`, `*`, `>`, `-`). → `{ kind:'snippet', text }`
3. else `rarestSighting(d)` → `{ kind:'species', text: commonName || scientificName }`
4. else `{ kind:'', text:'' }`

**Honest caveat to preserve in a code comment:** the first sentence is often the
dull opener ("Vis was poor at the start"). That weakness is *why* the optional
title exists and always wins the chain — don't try to make the auto-snippet
clever; the title is the curated path.

### 4b. Timeline cards (`renderTlRow`, history.js)

Type/register:
- `kind:'title'` / `kind:'snippet'` → **serif italic**, `--text-muted`; snippet
  keeps its `…`.
- `kind:'species'` → **upright bold sans**, `--text` (the existing rarest-name
  style). Signals "the creature" vs "your words".

**Desktop (`dh-*` strip):**
- Insert the glimpse as a line under `dh-site` (order: site → glimpse → meta).
  Data columns (depth/time/vis) and the marine cell are untouched — the glimpse
  lives in the identity column's whitespace.
- **Dive-number weight:** in `dh-sub`, bump `#{num}` to `--text` + bold (match
  mobile's `.dD-dnum`); keep `· {date}` muted.

**Mobile (`dD-*` card) — the rethink:**
- Move the species count up into the identity block beside the dive number:
  `#{num} · {N} sp.` (dnum bold `--text`, count muted). Fold the footage
  indicator in here too if present: `#{num} · {N} sp. ▶{k}`.
- The bottom marine row becomes: **thumb → glimpse → edit pencil**. The glimpse
  takes the freed width (2-line clamp). When `kind:'species'`, this is the
  rarest species *name* — which today's mobile card never showed, so even
  no-notes dives gain information (mobile/desktop parity bonus).
- Remove the now-redundant standalone `🐟 N sp.` from the marine row (it moved up).

### 4c. Dive-file notes placement (breakpoint-dependent)

The user decision: **desktop → below the map; mobile → unchanged in its Notes
tab.** Reconcile with the existing structure (desktop shows all panels as
columns; the `df-seg` tabs are mobile-only):

- **Desktop:** render `_notesBlockHtml(d)` in the **right column**, immediately
  after the map card (i.e. insert into `overviewContent` at index 1, before
  Conditions). Gives it above-the-fold visibility without touching the hero.
  Tag it (e.g. `df-notes--desk`) and **hide it `< 600px`**.
- **Mobile:** keep the dedicated `data-tab="notes"` panel (the Notes tab the user
  wants left alone), rendering the same `_notesBlockHtml(d)`. **Hide it `≥ 600px`**
  so it doesn't double with the desktop placement.
- Drop the `Notes` entry from the `df-seg` segmented control **only on desktop**
  is unnecessary — `df-seg` is already mobile-only, so the Marine | Overview |
  Notes tabs stay exactly as today on mobile. No change to the segmented control.

This dual-render via one helper is the only non-trivial layout piece; keep both
call sites pointed at `_notesBlockHtml(d)` so they never drift.

> ⚠ **The "below the map" placement above is provisional** — it assumes a short
> note. See §4d for the long-form layout problem, which must be resolved by design
> before this placement is final.

### 4d. Open design problem — long-form notes vs the two-column desktop layout (HANDOFF TO DESIGN)

**Do not solve this in this brief.** Captured here for handoff to a dedicated
design pass. The §4c desktop placement decision is provisional pending it.

**The problem.** The desktop dive file is a **two-column** layout: left = marine
(hero photo + species list), right = stacked metadata cards (map, conditions,
profile, gas & equipment, sign-off). §4c places notes in the **right column, below
the map** for above-the-fold prominence — which works *only while the note is
short*. But journal-length notes are the entire point of this brief: the persona
writes in the margins of a paper log, 4–5 paragraphs. A note that long in the right
column makes that column **far taller than the left**, shoving Conditions / Profile
/ Gas / Sign-off a long way down the page and leaving the left column ending in a
large empty gap — the two-column balance breaks. (The same breakage occurs if notes
stays in the *left* column, as in current production — just mirrored.)

So there is a direct tension between **prominence** (notes high, above the fold) and
**graceful variable height** (notes that may be empty → 5+ paragraphs) within a
fixed two-column grid.

**Constraints a solution must satisfy:**
- Notes is prominent / clearly discoverable — the journal is the point.
- Handles the full range empty → 5+ paragraphs **without** breaking column balance
  or pushing the metadata cards off-screen / unreachable.
- The metadata (conditions/profile/gas/sign-off) stays easy to reach.
- Consistent with the journal/serif treatment (§5) and the marine-first hero.
- **Desktop-only problem** — mobile keeps notes in its own scrolling Notes tab
  (§4c), which is unaffected. Don't regress mobile.

**Solution space is open** (e.g. a full-width notes band spanning beneath both
columns, an expand/"read more" affordance, a dedicated notes view, or an
independent-scroll metadata column) — **for design to evaluate, not decided here.**

## 5. Type system (new register)

Introduce a serif for the narrative layer (the app has none today):

- **Family:** Georgia / Times stack, e.g.
  `--serif: Georgia, 'Times New Roman', serif;` added to `:root`.
- **Italic** = short lifted aside → timeline glimpse only.
- **Upright** = full reading → dive-file title and body.
- Everything else (UI chrome, data, mono labels) is unchanged. The serif appears
  *only* where human prose does, which is the whole signal.
