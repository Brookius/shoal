The six ranks this app can actually record — plus the fallback
CREN VUNT LCDD LR/ntNA
EW and EX are gone, and VU took their place. This app logs sightings, so "extinct in the wild" and "extinct" can never legitimately be recorded — confirmed against the data rather than assumed: zero of the 1,275 rows in data/species-db.js carry either (LC 709, blank 376, VU 60, EN 39, NT 34, DD 33, CR 22). VU inherits EW's treatment — full neutral ink on a stronger tint — rather than EX's solid pale fill, which reads far louder than CR or EN and would invert the rank order visually.

That fixes a live AA failure, not just the token hygiene. The tracked item recorded VU's gold as "reads as compliant to a var() grep, isn't." Measured, light-mode VU is 3.33:1 — under AA outright. The neutral treatment takes it to 10.30:1 light / 8.86:1 dark. It also settles a disagreement between doc and code: the strict rule says VU stays plain Deep Water text, and neither the gold nor NT/LC's muted grey matched it. The hierarchy now follows the line IUCN itself draws — CR/EN/VU are the three threatened categories, NT/LC/DD are not — with CR and EN carrying hue, VU carrying full ink weight without hue, and the rest receding.

The vacated slot becomes a fallback, because two statuses had no rule at all. The DB holds one LR/nt and one NA. album.js:269 builds the class as 'iucn-' + iucn, giving .iucn-LR/nt — not even a valid selector, thanks to the / — and .iucn-NA, which is simply undefined. Both fall through to the base .iucn-badge, which sets no background, no border and no colour: bare text among chips. Moving the neutral treatment onto the base class fixes those two, removes the two dead rules, and closes the whole silent-fallthrough failure mode rather than one instance of it.

CR failed AA at the value that shipped. "CR holds rank through weight + stronger fill, not through darkness" is right, but implementing "stronger" as a brighter violet tint at α0.22 lifts the chip's background toward its own violet text and lands it at 3.74:1. Just dropping the alpha fixes contrast and inverts the rank signal (CR would be less filled than EN). Making the fill deeper instead — rgba(9,16,16,0.55), the theme's own darkest tone — gives 6.16:1 and reads visibly denser and heavier than EN's light tint. On dark, emphasis is subtraction of light, not addition.

NT, LC and DD were simply absent from the shipped block, and are fine once added — 6.10:1. Their light-mode counterparts hardcode rgba(139,123,106,0.1), a light-taupe literal that reads as compliant to a var() grep and isn't — the same class of bug the VU leak was, now the only one of that class left.
