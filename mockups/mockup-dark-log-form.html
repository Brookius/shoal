Notes on what changed here
The dive-type chips are the clearest case for --on-type. Selected chips fill with the ramp member and hardcode #fff; on the lifted ramp that runs 1.6–2.4:1. Deep ink gives 4.80:1 at worst. Unselected chips are unaffected — they carry the type only as a 4px left border on a neutral background, which is a graphical boundary, not text.

The segmented toggles are the counter-example, and worth having next to the chips: they fill with --accent-fill, which is constant across themes, so they keep white ink and look identical in both modes. That's the rule working as intended — the two controls sit inches apart and resolve their ink differently for a real reason.

The vis/temp dial gradients do not re-theme. Six baked literals (#16302F, #275C63, #3C7E9C, #9ecce7, #cadaea, #e9ba6a) encoding physical facts — water clarity and temperature — not UI state, so they'd be wrong to shift with the theme. But the vis dial's dark end is within a few points of the dark canvas and its edge disappears. Fixed with a box-shadow ring in --border-mid rather than by touching the gradient: the track needs a boundary on dark, not different colours. These six are separately flagged in the colour doc as undocumented; this is one more reason to register them.
